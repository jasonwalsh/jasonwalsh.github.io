# Deus caritas est — Part I Notes

## Eros (ascending)

- Today, the term "love" has become one of the most frequently used and misused words.
  - Especially in marriage, love between a man and a woman is seen as a glimpse of an irresistible promise of happiness.
- Eros, according to the Greeks, was an intoxication, an overpowering of reason by a "divine madness" tearing man from his finite existence to experience "supreme" happiness.
  - To me, this is the falsification of love.
- The Old Testament never rejected eros but fought against its warped and perverse nature, which it still has today. Its counterfeit divinization strips it of dignity and dehumanizes it.
- Prostitutes in the temple were seen as objects by those indulging in eros to satisfy selfish desires, while claiming "divine madness" influenced them. These prostitutes thus dehumanized their flesh as exploitation disguised as goddesses.
- We must catch ourselves when rising too high in eros, ascending to a state of "ecstasy," before becoming intoxicated by the notion of "divine power."
- Man becomes a commodity, something of use or value, by reducing eros to pure "sex." He sees his body (without soul) and sexuality as purely material.
  - The truth is, man can never truly experience himself without intimately uniting body and soul.

## Agape (descending)

- Conversely, agape is a concerned and caring love for the other, even to the point of sacrifice.
- Yet, eros and agape are not mutually exclusive. Together, both dimensions form a reciprocal love and cannot exist independently (a unity in duality).
- These two dimensions are not bound by time. Eros is an ongoing exodus from the closed, inward-looking self toward liberation through self-giving.
  - I particularly like Pope Benedict XVI's word choice here.
    - **Exodus:** Pharaoh was hard of heart and inward-looking. He ignored God's command to free His people despite the plagues. Even after the Passover and letting the Israelites go, Pharaoh pursued them in the desert. His demise came from refusing to relinquish possession over the Israelites (all ascending, no descending, intoxicated with "divine power").
    - **Liberation:** The act of God freeing the Israelites from the bondage of their taskmasters in Egypt.
    - **Ongoing:** Continuing without termination or interruption.
- > "Purification and growth in maturity are called for; and these also pass through the path of renunciation." (p. 5)
  - **Renunciation:** An act or instance of relinquishing, abandoning, repudiating, or sacrificing something, as a right, title, person, or ambition.
  - This statement debunks Friedrich Nietzsche's theory that Christianity "poisons" eros. Instead, it heals and restores its true quality.

## God's love for His people

- We see this unification of both dimensions of love in Scripture. For example, Moses was permitted to enter the tent of meeting to engage in direct dialogue with God ("one must constantly drink anew from the original source"). When he emerged, he could serve his people ("One can become a source from which rivers of living water flow").
- God's love for His people is so great that He turns against Himself. His love surpasses His justice.
  - One notion that comes to mind is the fall of man. The first people Christ frees from death are Adam and Eve, among the first of God's people created in His image and likeness.
- The Word Incarnate demonstrates this love by offering Himself up freely on the Cross for the salvation of humanity.

## God's love in the Most Blessed Sacrament (Eucharist)

- The instituting of the Holy Eucharist is the eternal covenant between God and man.
  - We cannot possess Christ for just ourselves.
  - We can belong to Him only in union with all those who have become, or who will become, His own.

# Part I Recap

In his 2005 encyclical *Deus caritas est*, "God is Love," Pope Benedict XVI explores two principal dimensions of love: eros and agape. He argues that forms of love differ substantially. For example, love for a family member is generally more intense than love for a career. The significance of love is shaped by the intimacy and emotion linked to a person or object.

Despite these distinctions, Pope Benedict XVI classifies love into two categories: eros, an ascending and self-oriented form, and agape, a descending, selfless, and generous form.

Although these forms of love may seem fundamentally different, Pope Benedict XVI asserts they are not mutually exclusive. When I first encountered this argument, I questioned its validity. How can eros, a self-seeking love, coexist with agape, which is sacrificial and humble? In his letter, Pope Benedict XVI gives examples of eros and agape working together. The example that resonated most was Moses. While leading the Israelites in the desert for forty years, Moses alone was permitted to enter the tent of meeting. This privilege allowed Moses to speak directly with God, exemplifying ascending love. After leaving the tent, Moses served the Israelites, demonstrating agape through sacrifice and service.

> One can become a source from which rivers of living water flow. Yet to become such a source, one must constantly drink anew from the original source, which is Jesus Christ, from whose pierced heart flows the love of God.

Integrating both dimensions forms the foundation of the reciprocal love God extends to humanity. In creating man, God recognized the profound need for agape and addressed it by taking a part of man to create woman. This act exemplifies self-sacrificial love and established the basis for the sacrament of Holy Matrimony, in which man and woman give themselves to each other.
